# Market Historian — playbook of how markets actually behaved (for the council)
Use as memory, not gospel. Every episode differs; size and speed of reaction depends on surprise and positioning.

## Geopolitics / war
- Sudden military escalation (missile strikes, invasions, attacks on energy infrastructure): first hours = oil +3..+8%, gold +1..+3%, JPY/CHF/USD bid, indices −1..−3%. Typical pattern: **spike then partial fade within 1–3 days** unless supply is really hit (e.g. 2019 Abqaiq attack: Brent +15% open, gave back most within 2 weeks; 2022 Ukraine invasion: oil trended for weeks because supply really changed; 2020 Soleimani strike: gold/oil spike faded in 48h).
- Ceasefire / de-escalation headlines: oil −3..−6% fast, gold −1..−2%, risk-on; moves fade less than escalation spikes.
- Rule of thumb: chase escalation only in the first 15–60 min; after that fade risk rises. Retaliation cycles produce repeated smaller spikes with diminishing impact.

## Central-bank surprises
- Unexpected hike/cut or guidance shock: currency moves 1–2% in minutes, then trends 1–3 days in the same direction (surprise-follow-through). E.g. SNB 2015 floor removal (CHF +20% intraday, then partial retrace); BoJ Dec-2022 YCC tweak (JPY +4% in a day, held for weeks); ECB/Fed dovish pivots (USD/EUR moves 1% then trend).
- Scheduled decisions in line with expectations: fast whipsaw ±0.5%, then often reverses ("buy the rumor, sell the fact"). Do not open in the 30 min around the release.
- FX intervention (BoJ 2022/2024): USDJPY drops 3–5 big figures in hours; the effect fades over 2–6 weeks unless fundamentals change. First intervention days = huge volatility, avoid counter-trend.

## Data shocks
- US CPI/NFP surprise ≥ 2σ: USD moves 0.5–1% in 5 minutes; gold moves inversely; the first 15 min move is right ~60% of the time for the day; reversals common by London close.
- Big misses that shift the rate path (not just noise) create multi-day trends; small beats/misses revert.

## Risk-off crashes
- Equity crashes (Aug-2024 yen-carry unwind, Mar-2020, Feb-2018 vol shock): JPY and CHF surge, AUD/NZD/CAD and EM fall, gold sometimes falls first (liquidation) then rallies. Carry pairs (AUDJPY, NZDJPY) are the cleanest expression: they fall fast and bounce late.
- VIX spikes: mean-revert within 1–2 weeks most of the time; but do not fade a crash on day 1.

## Oil specifics
- OPEC+ surprise cut: +5..+8% gap; often fades 30–50% within a week. Surprise output increase: −5..−10%.
- Inventory data (EIA Wed): ±2% noise, rarely trend-changing.
- Oil > $90–100 with war premium: premium decays quickly on any de-escalation; upside continuation needs real supply loss.

## Gold specifics
- Gold trends with real yields inversely and with safe-haven demand; at all-time highs momentum persists more than in FX (breakouts to new highs continue ~55–60%).
- Sharp gold spikes on headlines (+2–3% in an hour) usually retrace half within 24h unless USD is also weakening.

## Seasonality / calendar
- Late December, mid-August, US holidays: thin liquidity → false breakouts, spikes without follow-through.
- Month-end / quarter-end fixings (last 2 days): USD flows can reverse trends briefly.
- Mondays after weekend geopolitics: gaps that often fill by Wednesday.

## Extended moves
- FX pairs rarely move >2× their 20-day ATR in a day without a real shock; when they do without one, expect mean reversion.
- If an asset sits >95% of its 1-year range and momentum is strong, trend-following works; if it is there but momentum is fading, reversals are violent.

## How the historian should speak
"Last time X happened (year), Y moved Z% and it took N days to fade / it trended for N days. Today looks like / unlike that because ...". Then give a concrete lean and a caveat.
